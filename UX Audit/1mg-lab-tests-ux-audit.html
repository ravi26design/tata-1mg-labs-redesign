UX audit · tata1mg.com/labs · Lab Tests landing page

The desktop page sells the idea of a lab test. The mobile page sells the test.

The Lab Tests landing page is well-branded and trustworthy at first glance, but on desktop it shows no tests, no prices and no "most booked" starting points. Its three hero call-to-action chips each lead somewhere users are not prepared for, and the brand coral fails contrast wherever it carries text.

02

What was reviewed

Desktop and mobile serve two different pages under the same URL. Desktop is a category directory with a search bar; mobile is a merchandised feed with prices. Most of the critical findings come from that gap.

03

What works

  • One clear job above the fold. The H1 states the offer, the search sits directly under it, and the city is pre-selected. No competing hero carousel on desktop.
  • Trust signals are specific. NABL and CAP accreditation, "55+ cities", phlebotomist tracking and doctor-verified reports are concrete claims, not slogans.
  • The five-step "How it works" is genuinely sequential, so the numbering earns its place, and the photos show the real product (app, tracking map, sample bag).
  • Search suggestions are relevant. Typing "thyro" returned five thyroid tests and packages with no noise.
  • The auto-scrolling banner has a real pause control with an accessible name, which most Indian e-commerce sites omit.
  • The login modal is done properly: aria-modal, labelled input, background scroll locked, focus moved inside.
  • Mobile cards carry the right facts: price, MRP, test count and "Report in 7 hrs" answer the three questions a buyer asks first.
  • A single type family at a disciplined scale (12/14/16/18/28/40 px) keeps the page calm despite dense content.
04

Findings

Ordered by severity. Each finding states what was observed, why it matters to the person booking a test, and the recommended fix. Severity reflects impact on booking completion and on users with disabilities, not visual polish.

F-01Critical

Desktop shows no tests, packages or prices anywhere on the landing page

ObservedEvery desktop module is a category tile or a carousel of category tiles (Men, Women, Thyroid, CT Scan). The first price a desktop visitor can see is one click away. Mobile, by contrast, shows "Most booked health checkups" and "Recommended for you" with prices, discounts and Book buttons within two screens.

Why it mattersLab-test buyers arrive with a named test in mind or a budget question. A page that answers neither forces a second navigation before any commitment, and gives comparison shoppers nothing to anchor on. The desktop page is effectively a directory, not a storefront.

Recommendation

Bring the mobile "Most booked" and "Recommended" priced rows to desktop directly below the hero. Show the same card anatomy (name, test count, turnaround, price, Book). Category carousels can follow.

F-02Critical

"Book via Phone Call" opens a full-screen lead-capture form with no dialog semantics, no labels and no consent line

ObservedClicking the chip covers the whole viewport with a fixed layer titled "How can we help?" containing Name and Phone No.* fields and a Submit button. The layer has no dialog role, keyboard focus stays on the city field behind it, neither input is programmatically labelled (the visible labels are not associated), the phone field is not marked required in code, and there is no statement of when a call will come or how the number will be used.

Why it mattersUsers expected a phone number to call. Instead they are asked to hand over their own. Without a callback window or privacy note, many will abandon; screen-reader users will not know a form opened at all.

Recommendation

Lead with the toll-free number as a tap-to-call link, then offer the callback as a secondary option. Mark the overlay as a modal dialog, move focus to the first field, associate labels, set the phone field required, and add one line: "We'll call within 15 minutes, 8am to 8pm. Your number is used only for this booking."

F-03Critical

"Quick Order" is an unexplained label that dead-ends in a login wall

ObservedThe chip's only affordance is the words "Quick Order". Clicking it opens the OTP login modal with no message explaining what Quick Order is or why sign-in is needed. The app screenshot on the same page labels the equivalent action "Upload Prescription".

Why it mattersA hero CTA that demands login before revealing its purpose converts almost nobody who was not already going to log in. The name also fails the first-time visitor: "quick" relative to what?

Recommendation

Rename to "Upload prescription" with a one-line sub-label ("We'll add the tests for you"). Let users pick the file first and gate login at submission, with the modal saying "Sign in to save your prescription".

F-04High

Search has no submit control, no combobox semantics and nothing on focus

ObservedThe coral circle at the right end of the search bar is a plain image, not a button. The input sits in no form, has no submit, and carries no role, aria-expanded or aria-autocomplete. Focusing it shows nothing (no popular searches, no recent). Suggestions appear only after typing and show names only, with no price, type or "See all results" row.

Why it mattersThe most prominent control in the hero looks clickable and does nothing. Users who type a query and press the icon, or who cannot see the dropdown, have no way to run a search. Empty focus state wastes the moment of highest intent.

Recommendation

Make the icon a real submit button that opens the results page for the typed query. On focus, show "Popular: Full body checkup, CBC, Thyroid profile, HbA1c". Add price and a type tag to suggestions, plus a final "See all results for 'thyro'". Implement the ARIA combobox pattern.

F-05High

The brand coral fails text contrast wherever it is used as text or as a button ground

ObservedMeasured against WCAG AA (4.5:1 for text under 24 px):

  • Active nav item "LAB TESTS", coral #FF6F61 on white, 14 px: 2.73:1
  • "Get a free call back" button, white on #FF5443, 14 px: 3.18:1
  • Search placeholder and "+91", #868E9E on white, 16 px: 3.22:1
  • Toll-free number in green #308956 on white, 16 px: 4.25:1
  • Body grey #626A7A on white, 14–16 px: 4.9:1; ink #181A1F: 17:1

Why it mattersThe failing colour carries the page's current-location marker, its primary buttons and its search hint. Users with low vision or on a sunlit phone lose exactly the elements meant to guide them.

Recommendation

Keep coral for fills and icons, but darken it to about #D9432F for text and button grounds (passes 4.5:1 with white). Move the placeholder to #6B7484. Pair the active-nav colour with the existing underline so colour is not the only cue.

F-06High

A permanent sticky footer asks for a phone number on every scroll position

ObservedA 62 px fixed bar ("Need help? Call us at 1800-212-2323 or get a call back" with a phone input and a "Get a free call back" button) stays pinned to the bottom of the desktop viewport. The number is plain text, not a tel: link. The bar competes with the hero's own "Book via Phone Call" chip and repeats the same lead form.

Why it mattersOn a 720 px laptop viewport the bar removes 9% of the height for the whole session, and the third request for a phone number on one page reads as pushy rather than helpful. Two entry points to the same callback form split analytics and confuse the hierarchy.

Recommendation

Collapse it to a single "Need help?" pill that expands on click, or show it only after 60 seconds of inactivity or on exit intent. Make the number a tel: link. Remove the phone field from the bar and reuse the dialog from F-02.

F-07High

Page structure is not navigable by assistive technology

Observed

  • No header, nav or footer landmarks; the main landmark is nested inside another main. No skip link.
  • "Login" and "Signup" are divs with role="link" and no href, so they have no URL and rely on a script key handler.
  • Each category tile is a link that wraps a role="button" element: two interactive roles for one action.
  • 10 of 83 images have no alt text; the twelve category images use the literal alt "item_image".
  • The search input sets outline: none with no replacement, so keyboard focus is invisible on the page's most important control. Other controls rely on the browser default ring.
  • No prefers-reduced-motion rule exists for the auto-scrolling banner.

Why it mattersScreen-reader users cannot jump to navigation or content, cannot tell what a category image shows, and cannot see where they are when tabbing into search.

Recommendation

Wrap the bar in a header with a nav, mark the footer, keep one main. Make Login and Signup real links or buttons. Flatten tiles to a single anchor. Use empty alt for decorative images and the heading text for category images. Give the search input a 2 px focus ring in the ink colour. Pause the banner under reduced motion.

F-08High

The page is heavy and noisy, and mobile shows skeletons for a long time

Observed216 requests and 2.1 MB transferred on a fast connection, of which 105 requests are third-party; 54 script files and 10 tracking iframes. Load event at 3.3 s on broadband. The console logged 44 errors, almost all Content-Security-Policy violations from DoubleClick frames. On mobile, a capture taken 25 seconds after navigation still showed grey skeleton placeholders for the category and package images and a loading spinner at the bottom.

Why it mattersMost Indian lab-test traffic is mobile on variable networks. A feed of skeletons is a feed nobody can book from, and every tracker loads before the first package card.

Recommendation

Defer all tracking and ad frames until after first interaction or the load event. Lazy-load below-the-fold carousels but render the first two rows server-side with real images. Fix or drop the DoubleClick frame that violates the CSP.

F-09Medium

Carousel controls are detached from the rows they control

ObservedThe previous and next arrows for the "Tests and packages for your health needs" row sit at the far right, below the row and directly above the promotional banner, so they read as belonging to either. The "Radiology" row has no arrows at all. In the 1440 px capture the promotional banner was clipped at the left edge, cutting its headline to "…E KA BADGE".

Why it mattersUsers cannot tell there are more than six categories, and the clipped banner looks broken on the widest, most common desktop size.

Recommendation

Place arrows in the section header, right-aligned with the heading, on every carousel. Show a partial seventh tile as a scroll affordance. Re-check the banner track offset at 1440 px and above.

F-10Medium

Trust tiles look like cards but do nothing, and take half the hero

ObservedThe four photo tiles to the right of the search ("Trusted NABL certified labs", "Home sample collection", "Wide coverage across india", "Fast, accurate reports") are plain divs with no link, hover or cursor change. They occupy about 45% of the hero width at 1440 px.

Why it mattersRounded photo tiles with captions are the same visual grammar the page uses for clickable categories, so users click and nothing happens. The space could hold the priced rows missing in F-01.

Recommendation

Either link each tile to its proof (accreditation certificates, coverage map, sample report) or reduce them to a single-line trust strip with icons and give the hero width back to search and products.

F-11Medium

Labels and copy are inconsistent between desktop, mobile and the app

  • "Quick Order" (desktop) vs "Upload Prescription" (app screenshot) for the same action.
  • "Delivering to Gurgaon" on mobile describes a pharmacy, not a sample-collection service.
  • "Full body Packages" vs "Full Body Packages"; "Whatsapp" vs WhatsApp; "across india"; "Vitamins Tests".
  • Tile text breaks mid-phrase: "X Rays, Scans &" on one line, "More" on the next.
  • The SEO block says "45,00,000 lab tests booked" while the footer says "31m+ orders": two counting systems on one page.

Why it mattersEach inconsistency is small, but a healthcare buyer reads sloppiness in copy as sloppiness in the lab.

Recommendation

Keep one glossary for lab surfaces: "Collecting from Gurgaon", "Upload prescription", "WhatsApp", "Vitamin tests". Set tile labels with balanced wrapping or shorten them to "X-rays & scans".

F-12Medium

Mobile is a 7,000 px stack of twelve carousels with duplicated category navigation

ObservedThe mobile page runs roughly 18 screens: banner, category tabs (For Women, For Men, X-Rays), a second tile row for the same category, Most booked, Recommended, banner, Ultrasound/MRI/CT, banner, Fever, Lifestyle, Specialised, Athlete, Women care, Vaccination, How it works, trust strip. Every row scrolls sideways. A floating pink heart button sits over content just above the bottom tab bar, whose "Home" tab uses a back-arrow icon.

Why it mattersHorizontal carousels hide everything past the second card, and twelve of them mean most merchandising is never seen. Two ways to reach "For Women" within the first screen suggests the page lacks a decision about what it is for.

Recommendation

Cut to five sections: search and chips, Most booked (2-column grid, not carousel), Browse by concern (icon grid), Scans, How it works. Move the remaining rows to category pages. Replace the heart button with the "Need help?" pill and use a house icon for Home.

F-13Medium

Universal "50% off" anchor pricing weakens credibility

ObservedOn mobile, every full-body package shows a struck-through MRP and "50% off"; fever checkups "10% off"; single tests 9–11%. The MRP is set in 12 px grey, the smallest type on the page.

Why it mattersWhen every item is half price, the discount stops carrying information and starts reading as a sticker. Healthcare buyers are especially sensitive to "was" prices they cannot verify.

Recommendation

Lead with the price and what it includes ("86 tests · report in 7 hrs"). Show a discount only when it is time-boxed or member-specific, and say why ("Care Plan price").

F-14Medium

The city picker is a full A–Z list behind a 67 px wide field

ObservedClicking "Gurgaon" opens "Top Cities" (7) followed by an alphabetical list of every serviceable city from Abohar onward. The field itself is 67 px wide; the crosshair icon beside it (alt "Location current") has no visible label or tooltip.

Why it mattersTop cities help, but users elsewhere scroll a list of hundreds. The unlabelled crosshair is the fastest route and the least discoverable.

Recommendation

Type-ahead filter the list as the user types (the field already accepts input), add a "Use my location" text button, and show whether home collection is available in the chosen city right in the dropdown.

F-15Low

Phone numbers are not tappable

Observed1800-212-2323 in the sticky bar and 18002122323 in the SEO text are plain text, not tel: links.

Recommendation

Wrap both in <a href="tel:18002122323">. On desktop this also enables click-to-call from paired phones.

F-16Low

Footer social icons are below the minimum target size

ObservedSix social links measure 19 × 24 px, under the 24 × 24 px WCAG 2.2 minimum. All other controls on the page pass.

Recommendation

Pad each link to 32 × 32 px; the icons themselves can stay small.

F-17Low

WhatsApp and phone chips are script handlers with no URL

Observed"Book via Whatsapp" and "Book via Phone Call" are div elements with role="button" and a click handler; neither has an href. They cannot be opened in a new tab, bookmarked or tracked as link clicks.

Recommendation

Make WhatsApp a real link to the wa.me deep link with a pre-filled message, and phone a tel: link. Keep the chip styling.

F-18Low

The SEO accordion duplicates content and headings

Observed"Keep your health in check with Tata 1mg" expands to about 600 words with six H2 headings that repeat claims made above (certified labs, home collection, cities). It is collapsed by default, which is the right call.

Recommendation

Trim to the facts not stated elsewhere (2,000+ tests, 120+ labs, compare by price and rating) and demote its headings to H3 under one H2.

07

Method and caveats

The page was loaded in Chrome on 8 September 2026 with no login and Gurgaon as the default city. Structure, roles, labels, colours and sizes were read from the live DOM. Contrast ratios follow the WCAG 2.x formula against the nearest opaque background; text over photographs was excluded. Interactions (search typing, city picker, the three hero chips, login) were driven by script and their results read from the DOM, so hover states and animation timing were not observed. Full-page screenshots came from headless Chrome at 1440 px and 390 px; the mobile capture used an iPhone user agent, which serves 1mg's separate mobile-web page. Category, cart, checkout and post-booking flows are out of scope, as is testing with a real screen reader. Severity ratings are the reviewer's judgement and should be validated against booking-funnel analytics.
